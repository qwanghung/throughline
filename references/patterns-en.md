# English patterns to remove

Adapted for pitches from Humanizer (github.com/blader/humanizer, MIT), which builds on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing". The patterns are grouped by how often they show up in design pitches. "Weak alone" means fix it only when several show up together.

## Process

1. **Mark** every tell, strongest first.
2. **Draft** a rewrite. The original structure is not fixed.
3. **Check** the draft against this list and against the original claims: nothing lost, nothing invented.
4. **Write** the final version.

## Most common in pitches

1. **Re-explaining what the reader knows**: walking a PM through what onboarding is, or describing the client's own product back to them. Check it against the audience card.
2. **Sales language**: "seamless", "intuitive", "delightful", "game-changing", "elevates". Replace each with the behaviour that changes: "finishes checkout in 2 steps instead of 5".
3. **Inflated significance**: calling an ordinary fix "pivotal" or "transformative", or ending on vague optimism. State the size of the effect.
4. **"Not X but Y"**: rejecting something nobody proposed ("This isn't just a redesign; it's a rethink"). State Y.
5. **Arguing with no one**: inventing objections or straw options to knock down. Use the real strongest objection, or none.
6. **Process narration**: "First we conducted research, then we synthesised…". One sentence at most, and only if it builds trust.
7. **Borrowed authority**: "research shows" or "experts agree" with no source. Name the study, the session count, or the metric. Otherwise cut it.
8. **Vague connections**: "aligns with", "speaks to", "in line with our vision". Say the actual link with "because" or "so".

## Staging

9. **Run-ups before the point**: "Let's dive in", "So, here's the thing", "Honestly?".
10. **One-line closers and dramatic fragments** that repeat the point: "Simple. Fast. Done."
11. **Sayings that sound deep** in place of a specific claim: "Good design is invisible."

## Rhythm by rule

12. **Forced triads**: three adjectives or three items when the meaning needs one or two.
13. **Repeated sentence openings.**
14. **Dashes as the universal connector** (weak alone).
15. **Stacked qualifiers**: "potentially somewhat more" (weak alone).
16. **Passive voice with no subject**: "It was decided that…". Say who decided (weak alone).

## Inflation

17. **AI vocabulary**: "leverage", "robust", "holistic", "streamline", "empower", "unlock", "landscape", "journey" (when not a user journey), "crucial", "delve".
18. **Avoiding "is / has"**: "serves as", "boasts", "features", "stands as".
19. **Shallow "-ing" clauses** that tack on meaning: "…, ensuring a smooth experience".

## Formatting and leftovers

20. **Bold as decoration**, including bold labels on every list item.
21. **Decorative headings**: emojis, arrows, title case, topic names in place of claims.
22. **Chatbot residue**: "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if…".
23. **Writing about the document**: "This deck will walk you through…". Walk them through it instead.
24. **A heading repeated** in the first sentence of its section.
