# Dry run: "Glass and light" design-language doc

A real session, compacted into the steps it took. Use it to see how the skill is meant to be used. The result is live at https://claude.ai/artifact/3FmUpXMh5RLS3LAUbbAr2B. The working plan from that session is not included here.

**Starting point.** The designer shared a guide to the glass-and-prism visual language for an AI workflow map: "the writing isn't telling a good story, and there's too much over-explanation." They wanted to tell designers about a language born from transparency, translucency and the magic of light through a prism, with a little abstract storytelling.

---

## Step 1: The skill reads the material and guesses the audience

The skill read the whole page and filled in a draft audience card instead of asking blank questions:
- **Who:** designers building AI workflow tools.
- **What they know by heart:** glassmorphism, Liquid Glass, Figma's Glass effect, how refraction looks.
- **What they care about:** making AI work visible, a look that isn't another glass trend.
- **What they'll object:** "Isn't this just Liquid Glass?"

It also diagnosed the copy:
- **Every behaviour was described twice:** once as what you see, once as how it's drawn.
- **One section taught physics the audience already knows.**
- **The best line was at the very end:** "A viewer should see the system work before reading a word."

It asked three questions: who exactly, what should they do after reading, and where will they read it.

**The designer answered:** craft-obsessed designers who want to get AI slop out of their work and sharpen their taste. They should admire the work and clone it. They'll read it on the artifact, as a GitHub-style doc.

**What changed:** cloning made the how-it's-drawn detail valuable. The finding flipped from "cut it" to "move it into a folded *Build it* layer."

## Step 2: The skill states the conviction and ranks the reader's questions

- **Conviction:** "Craft-obsessed designers will clone this language once they believe its colour is earned."
- **Two through-line options:** both literal.
- **Seven questions in the reader's words, ranked:** "Isn't this just Liquid Glass?", "Why light and a prism?", "Can I rebuild it?"…

**The designer answered:**
- Both lines sound AI-generic because they're literal. A hook should be whimsical and imaginative, and make people curious.
- Two of the defensive questions weren't worth answering. Focus on putting each design decision into artistic words instead.
- The origin: sunlight through glass at home, casting a flare and a spectrum, and being dazzled by how magical light feels.

**What changed:** the hook and the through line were split into two jobs:
- **The through line stays plain and true:** "Light stays quiet until it meets glass."
- **The hook carries the imagination.**

The origin story was mapped onto the design part by part: the sun is Prism, the pointer is a smaller second light, the glass edge is the card's rim, the clear middle is where the text sits, a quiet window is a card at rest.

## Step 3: The skill plans before writing

The plan had:
- three hook candidates
- a structure for each decision: **Image → Rule → Build**, meaning one poetic line, the plain rule, then the exact values, folded
- the cuts: the physics lessons and the references section

**The designer answered:**
- Hook 3 ("a little spectrum on the floor") sounds odd and unrelatable.
- Hook 2 assumes every reader stares at sunlight the way the designer does.
- Hook 1 works, but without "beveled window".
- Keep the existing page structure, audit the story, keep value per minute high.

**What changed:**
- **The hook must work for any reader without the designer's backstory.** The personal story goes just after it, where it can be personal.
- **A rejected phrase is gone everywhere.**

## Step 4: The skill writes, in place

The skill kept the page's four parts and every live example, and rewrote only the words:
- **Hook (Concept heading):** "What if work could bloom the way sunlight does when it slips through glass?"
- **Origin, two short paragraphs:** the sunbeam at home, then the idea: work is the light, every module is a pane of glass, colour appears only where they meet.
- **Each behaviour:** one image line ("Touch, and the glass glows from your fingertip"), with the method and exact values folded under *Build it*. Every value was checked against the page's own code.
- **The Light model section:** cut from physics lessons down to one line each on what the map does. "Chromatic aberration" and "Dispersion" became one section. "Two lights" was removed because the Concept part already says it.
- **References:** moved to one line in the footer as vocabulary, which is what the original page said they were.
- **Removed repeats:** the "plays by itself; move your pointer in" note now appears once per part, and "Calm earns attention" was cut because it repeated "Quiet at rest".

Then it republished to the same artifact URL.

## How to use the skill, in short

1. **Say who you're convincing.** Bring your material if you have it; the skill drafts a guess for you to correct.
2. **Say the one thing they must believe or do.** The skill turns it into a conviction statement and the reader's real questions, ranked. Drop the ones that don't matter to you.
3. **Answer the top questions in your own words.** Your real moments and details are the raw material, and the skill won't invent them.
4. **React to the plan:** hooks, outline, cuts. Veto anything that doesn't sound like you. Vetoes stick.
5. **Approve, then it writes:** hook, point, proof, push, with a density pass and a pattern pass at the end.
6. **Refine:** review, tighten, adapt to Vietnamese, or rehearse.
